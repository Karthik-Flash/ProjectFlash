\title{FLASH: A Bit-Exact Integer CNN for Offline Chest X-Ray Pneumonia Screening on a Low-Cost Zynq-7020}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Karthikeya Reddy and Yashwant Rajesh}
\IEEEauthorblockA{BITS Pilani, Hyderabad Campus, India}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present FLASH, a pneumonia screener whose entire convolutional neural network runs in the
programmable logic of a low-cost Zynq-7020 (PYNQ-Z2) and reproduces an independent integer
reference model bit for bit. The network is trained directly in integer units on 224$\times$224
chest radiographs from the RSNA Pneumonia Detection dataset: five stride-2 3$\times$3
convolutions, global average pooling and a 64$\to$2 classifier, 47,602 parameters and 12.19
million multiply-accumulates per image. A single convolution engine, configured at run time by a
128-bit descriptor per layer, runs the same Verilog at 28$\times$28 and 224$\times$224. A chain of
three bit-exact links, from the trained network to a NumPy golden model to RTL simulation to the
board, ends with all 244 verification images matching on logits, margin and decision, on three
bitstreams. At 66.67\,MHz the engine takes 182.94\,ms per image, 99.97\% of its one-MAC-per-clock
peak. On 4,003 held-out patients the model reaches an AUROC of 0.8229 (95\% CI 0.8083--0.8379).
The first board run failed, 0/244 bit-exact, while 234 of 244 decisions still agreed. We
localised the cause offline from the board's own logits, a bus-width mismatch in the processor's
memory bridge, and confirmed it by flipping one register bit (0/244 to 244/244). The case shows
that decision-level agreement can hide arithmetic faults that logit-level bit-exactness catches.
We also report what the model does not do well: it confuses other lung abnormalities with
pneumonia, and part of its score comes from the film's projection. FLASH is a research prototype,
not a medical device.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Pneumonia killed 740,180 children under five in 2019, 14\% of all deaths in that age group
\cite{who2022pneumonia}. A chest radiograph is the usual confirmation, but it needs a reader,
and where readers are scarce a film can wait a long time for its report. Deep networks can read chest
radiographs \cite{wang2017chestxray8,rajpurkar2017chexnet}, but they usually run on a server, which
needs connectivity and sends images off site. A small device that reads the film where it is
taken, offline, and gives an immediate ``likely clear'' or ``read this first'' could help a
clinician prioritise.

We chose an FPGA for one main reason: the chip computes exactly the integers that were
validated, every time, with no operating system or floating-point library in between. That
property can be tested exhaustively and explained to a reviewer. It also makes integration
faults visible, because any deviation from the reference is a deviation in a 32-bit integer.

This paper describes FLASH V1, a research prototype on the PYNQ-Z2 board, and makes four
contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A bit-exact integer co-design and verification flow, from training to golden model to RTL
to board, at 224$\times$224 on a low-cost Zynq-7020 (Sections~\ref{sec:model},
\ref{sec:verif}).
\item A resolution-agnostic convolution engine driven by a layer table, which ran 28$\times$28
and 224$\times$224 with the same Verilog (Section~\ref{sec:hw}).
\item A board-only integration fault, localised offline from logits and confirmed by
intervention. It shows that decision-level agreement (234/244) hides arithmetic faults that
logit bit-exactness catches (Section~\ref{sec:afi}).
\item An honest clinical analysis: the model's confusion of other abnormalities (``Not Normal'')
with pneumonia, and a shortcut through the film's projection, AP or PA (Sections~\ref{sec:eval},
\ref{sec:disc}).
\end{enumerate}

We label key numbers as Measured (\meas), from a tool or board run on the current design;
Synthesis estimate (\synest), a Vivado estimate, which includes every power figure; or Derived
(\deriv), computed from measured values. The repository recomputes every derived number from the
raw evidence.

\section{Related Work}

\textit{Chest radiograph datasets and models.} ChestX-ray8 \cite{wang2017chestxray8} released
hospital-scale radiographs with text-mined labels, and CheXNet \cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet}
trained a 121-layer network on them for pneumonia. The RSNA Pneumonia Detection dataset
\cite{shih2019rsna} added radiologist annotations of possible pneumonia to a subset, with a
three-way label that separates ``Normal'' from ``No Lung Opacity / Not Normal''. Kermany
\textit{et al.} \cite{kermany2018} released paediatric radiographs, and MedMNIST v2
\cite{yang2023medmnist} includes a 28$\times$28 version of them (PneumoniaMNIST), which our
earlier prototype used.

\textit{Shortcut learning.} Zech \textit{et al.} \cite{zech2018} showed that pneumonia models
generalise unevenly across hospital systems and can pick up the source hospital itself. DeGrave
\textit{et al.} \cite{degrave2021} found COVID-19 radiograph classifiers relying on shortcuts
rather than pathology, and Geirhos \textit{et al.} \cite{geirhos2020} describe the general
phenomenon. We test one such shortcut directly: how well the film's projection alone predicts the
label.

\textit{Integer networks.} Jacob \textit{et al.} \cite{jacob2018} established
integer-arithmetic-only inference with quantization-aware training. FLASH trains directly in
integer units, so the exported integers \emph{are} the model. Global average pooling comes
from Network in Network \cite{lin2014nin}, and replacing pooling with strided convolution from
the all-convolutional net \cite{springenberg2015}.

\textit{FPGA CNN accelerators.} Zhang \textit{et al.} \cite{zhang2015} used a roofline model to
size compute and memory for CNN accelerators, Qiu \textit{et al.} \cite{qiu2016} mapped large
CNNs to an embedded Zynq with dynamic-precision quantization, FINN \cite{umuroglu2017finn}
generates dataflow accelerators for binarized networks, and hls4ml \cite{duarte2018hls4ml}
compiles small networks to low-latency HLS designs. Sze \textit{et al.} \cite{sze2017} survey
the metrics. These works target throughput with many parallel processing elements, on other
networks and resolutions, so a numerical comparison with FLASH would not be like for like. FLASH
deliberately uses one MAC per clock: its goal is verifiable exactness at clinical resolution on
a low-cost Zynq, with parallelism left as future work (Section~\ref{sec:v2}).

\textit{Reporting and regulation.} CLAIM \cite{mongan2020claim} and TRIPOD+AI
\cite{collins2024tripodai} set reporting standards for imaging AI and prediction models. We use
Wilson intervals \cite{wilson1927} for proportions, the bootstrap \cite{efron1979} for AUROC,
and the Hanley--McNeil standard error \cite{hanley1982} where only an AUROC and class counts
are available. Section~\ref{sec:disc} relates FLASH to IEC 62304 \cite{iec62304}, ISO 14971
\cite{iso14971}, ISO 13485 \cite{iso13485} and the FDA's AI/ML action plan
\cite{fda2021aiml}.

\section{Model and Integer Co-Design}\label{sec:model}

\textit{Data.} V1 uses the RSNA dataset \cite{shih2019rsna}: 26,684 adult patients, one
frontal radiograph each, stored as DICOM \cite{dicom}. We split by patient into 18,678 training,
4,003 validation and 4,003 test patients; test prevalence is 22.5\%. Preprocessing applies the
modality look-up table and the window, inverts MONOCHROME1 images, letterboxes to a square,
area-resizes to 224$\times$224 and rounds to 8 bits. It passes seven self-tests, including real
scanner files (JPEG 2000, MONOCHROME1, modality LUT before window).

\textit{Network.} Five 3$\times$3 convolutions with stride 2 and padding 1 take the channels
1$\to$8$\to$16$\to$32$\to$48$\to$64. Global average pooling (GAP) reduces each of the 64 final
maps to one value, and a dense layer maps them to two logits (Table~\ref{tab:net}). Because GAP
removes the spatial size, the classifier and the parameter count do not depend on the input
resolution.

\textit{Integer rules.} Training, the golden model and the hardware share one arithmetic.
Pixels and activations are unsigned 8-bit, weights signed 8-bit in $[-127,127]$, biases 32-bit.
A convolution output is the bias plus the sum of weight$\times$pixel over the 3$\times$3 window
and all input channels. It is shifted right by a per-layer amount (7, 7, 7, 8, 8) and clamped to
0..255; the clamp is the ReLU. GAP sums each 7$\times$7 map and shifts right by 6, dividing by 64
instead of 49, which costs a wire rather than a divider. The decision uses the margin
$m=\text{logit}_1-\text{logit}_0$: an image is flagged when $m>T$.

\textit{Accumulator sizing.} The accumulator width comes from the trained weights. For each
output channel, $\sum\max(w,0)\cdot255+b$ and $\sum\min(w,0)\cdot255+b$ bound the sum for
\emph{any} 8-bit image. The widest layer needs 22 bits, and the margin 19 bits. The range observed
on 8,006 validation and test images lies well inside that bound. An overflow would be a silent
wrong answer, so we size for any input, not for the dataset.

\textit{Training.} Weights are trained directly in integer units: first unconstrained (24
epochs), then with rounding on every forward pass and a straight-through gradient (16 epochs).
The epoch with the best validation AUROC is kept (0.8380). The threshold $T=-646$ was chosen on
the validation set for at least 90\% sensitivity, then locked before the test set was examined.
On the 4,003 test patients the model reaches AUROC 0.8229 (bootstrap 95\% CI 0.8083--0.8379),
sensitivity 91.0\% (89.0--92.7) and specificity 53.9\% (52.1--55.6)\meas.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the system. The ARM core copies an image into a DMA buffer, writes
the threshold and a start bit, starts an AXI DMA \cite{pg021} in simple mode, polls a status
register, and reads back two logits, the margin, the decision and a cycle count. It never
computes any part of the network.

\textit{The core.} \code{layer\_seq} walks a ROM of layer descriptors, one 128-bit word per
layer. Each holds the operation, kernel, stride and padding (4 bits each); input and output
channels, heights and widths (12 bits each); the shift (6 bits); and the weight and bias base
addresses (20 and 10 bits). It starts the right unit, swaps the two feature-map buffers between
layers and streams the GAP input. \code{conv\_engine} is shared by all five convolutions. It is
pipelined as operand multiplexer, register, multiplier, register and accumulator, and does one
MAC per clock. Padding is a gated read: an out-of-image tap contributes zero, so no padded frame
is stored. \code{gap\_unit} keeps 64 running sums, \code{fc\_unit} computes two 32-bit logits,
and \code{decision} compares the margin with a run-time threshold register.

\textit{Scaling from 28$\times$28 to 224$\times$224.} The audited predecessor (v0) was a fixed
28$\times$28 design with one hand-written module per stage. Its classifier flattened a
4$\times$14$\times$14 map into a 784$\to$16 dense layer, so 99.7\% of its 12,634 parameters sat
in layers whose size grows with the image\deriv. At 224$\times$224 its first dense layer alone would
need 802,816 weights\deriv. In V1, moving from 28$\times$28 (stage V1.1) to 224$\times$224 (V1.2)
needed only new memory files (weights, biases, layer table) and one RTL change: the feature-map
buffers grew from 64\,KB to 128\,KB, because conv1's output is 8$\times$112$\times$112 = 100,352
bytes. The descriptor layout is identical at both sizes. The cost is in work and memory. MACs per
image grow from 227,360 to 12,192,896, $\times$53.6\deriv. That is less than the $\times$64 pixel
ratio, because the small maps at 28$\times$28 round up (7$\to$4$\to$2$\to$1). Block RAM grows
from 48.5 to 80.5 tiles for the core alone\synest. The convolution's address arithmetic
($c\cdot h\cdot w + y\cdot w + x$, a DSP multiply-add into the block-RAM address) is the
critical path at both sizes.

\textit{The AXI wrapper.} \code{top\_v1\_axi} accepts the image as 32-bit AXI-Stream beats,
four pixels per beat, 12,544 beats per image, and feeds the core one pixel per clock. It
reproduces the exact drive sequence of the simulation testbench: threshold, all 50,176 pixels,
one idle cycle, one start pulse. It exposes the registers of Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}, with VERSION =
0xF1A50102 and CYCLES counting core clocks from start to result.

\section{Verification Methodology}\label{sec:verif}

FLASH's claim rests on three bit-exact links, each a comparison of integers, not a tolerance.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Trained network = golden model.} The golden model is an independent NumPy program
in int64 that reads only the exported files. It gives logits identical to the training framework
on all 4,003 test images, with 0 mismatches on every intermediate tensor of the 244 verification
images\meas.
\item \textit{Golden model = RTL.} The simulator dumps every layer's output and a script compares
it with the golden model, so a mismatch points to one layer. At 224$\times$224, 16 of 16 images
and 12 of 12 per-layer traces are exact in Vivado xsim\meas. The AXI wrapper passes a 33-check
testbench with varied bus timing and error cases\meas. An independent audit re-ran the golden model
against all 244 vectors and 60 traces of both stages, with 0 mismatches.
\item \textit{RTL = board.} The board compares logit0, logit1, margin and decision with the
expected files on all 244 images (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Unbiased vectors.} The 244 verification images are a seeded, balanced slice of the test
split, 122 pneumonia and 122 not. They were drawn without looking at the model's confidence, and
the model gets 70 of them wrong at $T$. The hardware must reproduce those mistakes too, because a
matching 32-bit logit cannot happen by accident. FLASH's hackathon predecessor compared one
decision bit on eight images picked because the model was confident about them; three datapath
bugs survived five versions of it.

\textit{Bug ledger.} Table~\ref{tab:bugs} lists every defect found, by phase. Two of them (11
and 15) would have produced a confidently wrong device with no error message.

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:impl}

The design targets the xc7z020clg400-1 with Vivado 2022.2. The block design holds the processing
system, the AXI DMA (read channel only), \code{top\_v1\_axi} as a module reference, a reset
block, and in one variant an AXI GPIO for the board LEDs. The DMA reaches DDR through the HP0
port. Vivado will not connect the AXI4 DMA directly to the AXI3 port, so a protocol converter
sits between them.

\textit{Clock.} The fabric clock is 66.67\,MHz (1000\,MHz/15). The processing system cannot
synthesise 75\,MHz exactly (a 75\,MHz request gives 76.92\,MHz), and the next step, 71.43\,MHz,
failed post-route timing with WNS $-0.925$\,ns\meas. At 66.67\,MHz the critical path, from the
convolution's address register through two DSP slices and one LUT into a block-RAM address pin,
takes 13.786\,ns of the 15\,ns budget, 70\% of it logic\meas. The RTL was frozen for the board work,
so we accepted the lower clock; pipelining that path is a V2 item.

Table~\ref{tab:impl} gives the post-route results. V1.2.1 differs from V1.2 only in the HP0 and
DMA data width; the LED variant adds the GPIO. The accelerator's netlist is identical in all
three: its 2,274 memory-initialisation lines match.

\section{Case Study: A Fault Only the Board Could Show}\label{sec:afi}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_afi_case.pdf}
\caption{Left: the first board run, V1.2, against the expected margins (Measured). The logits
correlate at $r=0.98$ and 234 of 244 decisions agree, yet no image is bit-exact. Right: a crop of
image 0 as sent and as the PL received it under the dup\_even model (Derived). Each 64-bit group
keeps its first four pixels and repeats them, a mild horizontal blur that GAP largely tolerates.}
\label{fig:afi}
\end{figure*}

\textit{Symptom.} The first V1.2 bitstream returned 0/244 bit-exact. Nothing else looked wrong
(Fig.~\ref{fig:afi}): the logits correlated with the expected ones at 0.98, 234 of 244 decisions
agreed, the result was identical at 25, 50 and 66.67\,MHz (ruling out timing), repeated runs and
soft resets gave identical outputs, and the DMA reported no error. A test that compared
decisions would have passed this hardware.

\textit{Offline localisation.} Rather than guess at the RTL, we used the golden model as an
instrument and sent the board synthetic probes. Constant images and an image constant along each
row came back exact; a ramp along each row came back wrong. That pointed at pixel order within a
row. We searched candidate corruptions (byte orders within a beat, row shifts, flips, block
reversals, beat permutations, padding variants) for one that reproduces every board output.
Exactly one did: each odd 32-bit input word, four pixels, is replaced by the even word before it
(``dup\_even''). It reproduces the board on 244 of 244 images and 24 of 24 probes, where the
unmodified golden model matches only 12 of the 24 probes\deriv. A word pair never crosses a 224-pixel
row, which is why row-constant probes were immune.

\textit{Prediction before observation.} For the second probe set, the model's outputs were written
down before the board's results were examined. Two single-pixel probes discriminate. For a bright
pixel at (1,0), dup\_even predicts $(-349, 102, 451)$ for (logit0, logit1, margin), where the
golden model predicts the all-zero image's $(-375, 106, 481)$; for a bright pixel at (223,223) the
predictions are reversed in kind, the all-zero result against $(-386, 114, 500)$. The board
matched the dup\_even predictions exactly\meas.

\textit{Mechanism and intervention.} The DMA reads DDR through the HP0 port, whose AFI bridge has
its own width setting, bit 0 of its RDCHAN\_CTRL register \cite{ug585}. V1.2 used a 32-bit HP0,
but PYNQ boots the processing system with the AFI in 64-bit mode and does not run the design's
own initialisation code, so each 32-bit read returned the low half of a 64-bit word, the even
word. On one boot, the 32-bit bitstream gave 0/244 (run 2); after writing that single bit from 0
to 1 it gave 244/244 (run 3). Nothing else changed: this is a causal test, not a fit.

\textit{Fix and lesson.} V1.2.1 makes HP0 and the DMA's memory side 64-bit, matching PYNQ's
default, and needs no register write (run 1). Every board notebook now compares the AFI setting
with the bitstream's hardware description and refuses to run on a mismatch. The simulations
drove the stream directly and never modelled the processor's memory path, so they could not see
this fault. The general lesson is that a classifier tolerant to input corruption will pass
decision-level tests while computing the wrong function. Comparing integers end to end is what
exposed it.

\section{Discussion: Clinical Readiness and the Regulatory Gap}\label{sec:disc}

FLASH shows that the integer network runs exactly in hardware. It does not show that the network
is clinically useful. An AUROC of 0.82 on one public adult dataset is research grade. The model
separates pneumonia from normal lungs well, but at $T$ its test-set specificity is 33.4\%
against other abnormalities, against 81.3\% against normal lungs\meas, and a screener in a real
clinic will see such abnormalities constantly. Part of its score comes from the projection, which
partly reflects how unwell the patient was when the film was ordered, since AP films are often
taken at the bedside; this is the kind of shortcut that \cite{zech2018,degrave2021,geirhos2020}
warn about. Its PPV depends on prevalence, which the deployment site, not the test set, determines.

Reporting guidelines make the gaps concrete. CLAIM \cite{mongan2020claim} and TRIPOD+AI
\cite{collins2024tripodai} ask authors to state, among other things, the intended use, how the
model was validated, including on external data, and how it performs across subgroups. FLASH reports subgroups
and a locked, validation-chosen operating point, but it has no external or paediatric
validation, no comparison with radiologists, and no prospective data. Pneumonia mortality is
concentrated in children \cite{who2022pneumonia}, while RSNA is adult.

Regulation adds process on top of evidence. A device would need a software life-cycle process
(IEC 62304 \cite{iec62304}), risk management (ISO 14971 \cite{iso14971}) and a quality
management system (ISO 13485 \cite{iso13485}). The FDA's action plan \cite{fda2021aiml} frames
how AI/ML-based software that changes over time should be managed. FLASH's bit-exactness helps
with one narrow part of this, verification: the deployed artefact provably computes the
validated function, and a changed threshold is a register value rather than a new model. It does
nothing for clinical validity. We therefore describe FLASH as a research prototype and not a
medical device.

\section{Future Work (V2)}\label{sec:v2}

The project pauses after V1. Three items would make the board, rather than software, the source
of every number:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{A board run on all 4,003 test images.} The 4,003-image accuracy is the golden
model's, bit-exact to the board on 244 images; a full board run (about 12 minutes of compute)
would make it literally the board's output.
\item \textit{Measured board power.} Power so far is only a Vivado estimate.
\item \textit{DICOM end to end on the board.} DICOM preprocessing has only been verified on the
PC.
\end{itemize}

Beyond those, the model needs a clinical operating point: explicit treatment of the Not-Normal
class, per-projection analysis, and a threshold chosen on validation data for a stated
specificity as well as sensitivity. Since $T$ is a register, that needs no hardware change.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_v2_projection.pdf}
\caption{Derived projection of compute latency against MACs per clock, from the measured cycle
count. It assumes ideal scaling of the MAC cycles, fixed non-MAC cycles and sufficient memory
bandwidth. 75\,MHz assumes a pipelined address path.}
\label{fig:v2}
\end{figure}

On the hardware side, only 14 of 220 DSP slices are used. Several MACs per clock need wider or
parallel feature-map reads, since the engine now reads one byte per clock; a line-buffer window
generator is already built and tested for this. Figure~\ref{fig:v2} projects the gain. At 16 MACs
per clock the compute time would be 11.5\,ms at 66.67\,MHz and 10.2\,ms at 75\,MHz\deriv, still not
below the desktop PC's 8.7\,ms. The case for the FPGA must therefore rest on measured power,
determinism and cost, not on speed alone, which is why measured power is one of the three
evidence items above.

\section{Conclusion}

FLASH runs a 224$\times$224 integer CNN for pneumonia screening entirely in the programmable logic
of a Zynq-7020 and reproduces an independent reference model bit for bit on all 244 verification
images, across three bitstreams. A layer-table-driven engine let the same Verilog scale from
28$\times$28 to 224$\times$224 with new memory files and one buffer size. The most useful
lesson came from a failure: a board-only bus-width fault left 234 of 244 decisions intact while
corrupting every logit. Logit-level exactness exposed it, the golden model localised it offline,
and a one-bit intervention proved it. The model itself is honest but modest, and its weaknesses,
the Not-Normal confusion and the projection shortcut, are measured and published with it. The
design, evidence and every derived number are available at
\url{https://github.com/Karthik-Flash/ProjectFlash}.
